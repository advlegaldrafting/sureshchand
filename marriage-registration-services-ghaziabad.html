Why Is Marriage Registration Compulsory in India?

According to the landmark judgment passed by the Supreme Court of India, registering a marriage is now mandatory for all citizens across all religions and communities. Earlier, traditional or religious ceremonies like those under Hindu customs were considered sufficient socially, but they lacked formal government records. A registered marriage creates an undisputed legal proof of your relationship, protecting the legal rights of both spouses in matters of inheritance, property, and matrimonial laws.

The Legality: Hindu Marriage Act vs. Special Marriage Act

The registration process in Ghaziabad generally falls under two major legal frameworks depending on the situation of the couple:

  • The Hindu Marriage Act, 1955: This Act applies when both the husband and wife belong to Hindu, Sikh, Buddhist, or Jain religions, and their marriage has already been traditionally solemnized (e.g., through Vedic rites or Arya Samaj rituals). The registration under this act is relatively faster.
  • The Special Marriage Act, 1954: This Act is applicable for civil marriages where the couple belongs to different religions or nationalities, or if they wish to solemnize and register their marriage directly before the Marriage Officer without a religious ceremony. This process involves a mandatory 30-day public notice period.
